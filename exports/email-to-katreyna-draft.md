Dear Katreyna,

When we spoke in June you kindly offered to give feedback on the maturity model
underpinning the DAR work. The framework has moved on considerably since the
Bhutan version, and it is now at a point where your review would be most
valuable — before we lock it in for the country pilots.

I attach two documents:

1. **DAMM v1.5 — Guidebook.** A short guide to the model as it now stands: what
   it measures, how a reading becomes a level, how the three read-outs are
   computed, and what the model deliberately refuses to do.

2. **DAMM v1.5 — the model itself**, as a workbook. The full indicator set with
   pillars, weights, thresholds, the written anchors for qualitative
   indicators, the core gates and the scoring rules.

Where the model now stands: 102 indicators across 8 pillars, 14 core gates
treated as prerequisites rather than tradeable points, and three separate
read-outs — capability, ecosystem and outcomes — which the model refuses to
average into a single number. Roughly half the indicators are quantitative
lookups; the other half are qualitative and need an assessor's judgement
against written anchors. That split matters for how we resource a country
assessment, and it is the main thing I would like your view on.

Two points where your steer would be particularly helpful:

- **The indicator set.** Is anything missing that a task team would expect to
  see, and is anything there that we could drop without weakening the
  diagnostic? It has grown to 102 and I would rather prune it now than later.

- **The prohibitions.** The model refuses cross-country ranking, refuses to let
  a stage serve as a PDO indicator or disbursement condition, and withholds any
  maturity stage until a human has validated the evidence. I have built these
  into the software rather than into guidance. I want to be sure that line sits
  where the Bank would want it.

On testing: the model has been run end to end on Egypt, and the results are
consistent with the worked assessment. Nigeria and Uzbekistan would both be
good next candidates, and I am happy to align with the Nigeria DAR team
whenever suits them.

No rush on this — whenever you have time to look would be fine, and I am glad
to walk you through it on a call if that is easier.

With best regards,

Randeep
